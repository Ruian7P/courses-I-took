## 1. Early Japan and Naming History

- Jomon -> Yayoi
  - Jomon: pre-agricultural hunter-gatherers, original Japanese people
  - Yayoi: rice agriculture introduced from Asian continent --> shaped Japanese society
- **Wa 倭 -> 和** and **Yamato 大和**
  - Wa: the oldest Chinese name of Japan, meaning dwarf; later replaced by 和
  - Yamato: big Wa
    - the biggest tribe colonize Japan during Yamato period
    - Yamato Kotoba / Wago: the ascient Japansese language
- **Nihon 日本**
  - changed during Nara Period
  - origin of the sun: land of the rising sun



- **Yamato**
  - idea of Japan begins
  - myths
  - time / history: divine temperality: beyond the time



## 2. Nara Period

- **Nara**: First permanent capital, modeled on Changan
  - **stability**, center

- Political structure adopted from China: 
  - Confucian bureaucratic logic
  - Buddhism as state religion: regent use buddhism for legitmacy, and buddhist temple relies on regent family for money and political status
- **Manyoshu**: Oldest anthology of Japanese poetry, written in Chinese characters, but just for pronouciation
  - Shows unity vs difference



## 3. Heian Period

more sense of **permanence** and **stability**

- Capital moved to Heian (Kyoto) to escape the growing political power of Buddhist monasteries in Nara
- **language**
  - scripts
- **Emperor's Power**
  - retained symbolic and ritual 
  - real power often shifted to aristocratic families: Fujiwara married daughters to emperors to access the power
- **Culture**
  - **The Tale of Genji**
    - the world's first novel
    - explores aesthetics vs morality, and the issue of legitmacy
  - **Fukinuki Yatai (art style)**: blown-off roof, let viewers see interiors of aristocratic life
  - Kimono
  - **Hojoki**: world is unstable, self withdraws, morality is detachment, simplicity, and sufficiency



## 4. Kamakura: Rise of Samurai

**unstable**, **decentralized**, **emperor begins to be irrelevant**

- starts on the mid-Heian

- **shift in power**: Emperor lost control of distant provinces. Clan becomes more powerful. Local clans gained power through land and warrior

- **Gokenin Home**: Family as a unit of power

  - **Gokenin**: lord provides military service and land management in exchange for privileges and land rights for shogun

  - **Samurai**: to serve

    - **Ji-samurai**: samurai to farmer, farm to feed family. Land = basis of military power

  - **ie(家)**: household or family unit. **Patriarchal (父权制)** but collective

    

## 5. Ashikaga / Sengoku : Warring Period

- Onin War: civil war, collapse of central Ashikaga authority

- Sengoku Era: constant conflict among local lords (daimyo)

  - **Gekokujo (下剋上)**: the low overturn the high. Even peasants or lower lords can rise by force

  - Unifier

    - Oda Nobunaga 织田信长
    - **Toyotomi Hideyoshi 丰臣秀吉**: completed much of unification, invaded Korea twice, castle-building (stone bases = new symbol of centralized power).

    - **Tokugawa Ieyasu 德川家康**: defeated rivals at the **Battle of Sekigahara (1600)**, established the Tokugawa Shogunate.

## 7. Meiji Period

nation starts here for the emergence of nationalism

mix of old and new: Restoration + Renovation

- Politics and Ideologic: New nation with **contradictory sovereignty**
  - sovereignty: rights to rule
  - Constitution claims government **power from the people** (democratic), but only 2% of people can vote
  - Emperor simultanuously gifts the people the constitution as a god, he has the rights to rule
  - Constitution claims everybody is same, but Emperor stands above everybody (as a god, not a commoner at all)
  - kokutai -> one biological "body", patriarchal hierarchy
  - **Nation vs Empire (two logics)**
    - Nation: homogeneity, fixed boarder, citizenship, single language/culture
    - Empire: hierarchy, expanding borders, multiple poeple/language
    - Japan claims to be both -> contradictions (eg. Koreans in Japan were labor without citizenship)
  - Idea of **Nation-State**
    - **Nation**: (sameness), race, culture, language, history
    - **State**: (borderness), region, population, institutionalized government 

- Economy, Industrialization

  - monetary
  - centralized redistribution of surplus --> industrialization
  - top-bottom: industrial capital, based on monopolies --> zaibatsu (财阀) --> industrial capitalism
  - bottom-up: all kinds of people 

- Language

  - Genbun itchi: a movement that unify spoken and written language
  - language treated as rational technology
  - not use Chinese kanji

- Education

  - early education system: open, liberal, you can decide what it means to live a profitable life
  - 1890 Imperial Rescript on Education: students were required to memorize patriotism, loyalty, fillial piety
  - Risshin vs Shushin (contradictory attitudes and policies)
    - Risshin: do whatever you want (freedom)
    - Shushin: do what you will (self-discipline), where education can help you

- Westernization

  - early stage: pure adoption (Western-style dress, dance)

  - later: translate modernity rather than pure adoption; culture comes back as critique of techonology / capital

  - Fukoku kyohei (富国强兵): enrich the country, strengthen the military

## 8. Taisho Period

**Taisho Democracy**: open, liberal, cultural (culture over politics)



- **Ideology**

  - **modernize without westernize at all** (return to Asia); questioning whether modernization means westernization

  - **Make this new nation homogeneous in seemingly natural way**

    1. **Jomin grounds the history for national unity of Japanese People (seeming natural but actually cultural way) ** 

       - **problem**: New modern nation turns its back on real traditions and pasts. But it still needs tradition to make it legitimate and seems it was always there. How to ground the nation and people if you've rejected the past?

       - **solution**: invents **Jomin** to show the enduring essence of Japanese

         - **Jomin** (the folk, 常民) by Yanagita Kunio

           - **enduring**: it has a historic past (no matter when and how it change), transcends time and history

             - **homogenous**: **national unity** for all Japanese People instead of **space of difference**
             - **natural**: not clear as skin color, blood is part of the idea of living as one body, living as part of the body of the emperor
             - **contradictory**: it endures (not changed) --> contradicted to the fact that history will change, where essence of Japanese might just be created during recent age

             claimed to be natural / eternal essence, but in fact historical 

             - **ambiguous**: study **spaces of difference** of ethnics in the margins (Okinawa, Ryukyu, Ainu) as critiques on the modern Japan / modernity, yet use Jomin to ground **national unity** of Japanese

    2. **Physical differences in people in colony brings natural boundaries for Japanese People (in natural way), empire makes cultural differences with other nation feel natural**

       - race globally
         - fictive 
         - good category for thinking centralized difference
         - **good grounding for stabilizing identity** (an identity goes with a nation)
         - **supplements national identity** (not define a nation's boundaries or space)
       - **Minzoku**: sometimes race, simply mean ethnicity (customs like belief, language)
         - race is natural, while ethnicity is not natural
         - Minzoku/Jomin ambiguous between race and enthinicy
         - Jomin more emphasize Japanese people's uniqueness in a seemingly natural way, while Minzoku is more generic category for each ethnicity

       - colonial others introduces physical difference --> logic of race (natural category of difference) brought into the claim of identity
         - Empire based on natural difference, while Nation based on cultural difference
         - Empire makes cultural differences with other nation feel natural

    

    - **Purpose** (why pursuing natural species identity)
      - legitimacy for this nation
      - national unity of Japanese People
      - defend themselves against other colonial people within their empire

    

- **Culture**

  - diversified, could be emerged from modernity, could be against modernity

- **Gokenin** (御家人)

  Gokenin is the direct vassal bound to the shogun by military service and administrative duties, during the late Heian Period to Tokugawa Period. During the Tokugawa period, a gokenin has no sovereign domain. It signals the household based power structure during Tokugawa Period

  Gekokujo means the lower overturning the upper. It's a pattern during the Sengoku Period. It signals the breakdown of fixed hierarchy and force-based social mobility where even peasants or lower lords can rise by force during that period

- **Shogun**

  Shogun is the truly military and executive ruler of Japan from Kamakura through Tokugawa Period, while the emperor remained symbolic-ritual figure. They started Tokugawa period with their bakuhan system, which centralized his power in Edo and decentralized the power in the emperor and Daimyos

- **Daimyo**

  Daimyo are the lords who ruled their own han (domains) from the Sengoku through Tokugawa. They have their sovereign. Types included fudai (Tokugawa allies) and tozama (outsider, often former rivals). They are under the bakuhan system, where they have their local autonomy under shogun's control via Sankin-kotai and one castle rule ...

- **Kogi**

  Kogi means great leader of the public. It's a claim of legitimacy during the Tokugawa period. It frames the shogun as the great leader ruling on behalf of the public. It embodied centralized shogun authority within the bakuhan system

- **Tendo**

  Tendo means the way of Heaven. It's a claim of legitimacy during the Tokugawa period. It frames the Tokugawa authority as aligned with heaven and natural rule from the Confucianism and Buddhist idea. It embodied centralized shogun authority within the bakuhan system

- **Sakoku**

  Sakoku is the policy of "closed country" during the Tokugawa period. It banned overseas travel and tightly regulated foreign contact. It shows the fixity and hierarchy of the bakuhan system

  Sankin Kotai is the alternate attendance system during the Tokugawa Period. It requires Daimyo to live in Edo part of the year in regular cycle, and their family continue living in Edo as hostage.   Eta are the people at the bottom of the Tokugawa Social Stratification, meaning full of dirt. They often do dirty job like leatherwork and execution.
